I'm organizing a 3-day technical conference and need to schedule talks into rooms and times.

Venue and time grid:
- 6 rooms of varying capacity:
  - Main Hall: 300 seats, has a stage and projector setup for keynotes, also has live-streaming equipment
  - Room Alpha and Room Beta: 150 seats each, standard projector and microphone setup
  - Room Gamma, Room Delta, and Room Epsilon: 50 seats each, smaller breakout rooms with whiteboard and screen
- 7 one-hour times per day:
  - Morning block: 9:00, 10:00, 11:00
  - Lunch break: 12:00-13:00 (no talks, but the Main Hall is used for a sponsored lunch session on Day 2)
  - Afternoon block: 13:00, 14:00, 15:00, 16:00
- Walking time between rooms: rooms on the same floor are 2 minutes apart, rooms on different floors are 5 minutes apart. Main Hall and Alpha/Beta are on floor 1, Gamma/Delta/Epsilon are on floor 2

Talks and speakers:
- 80 talks total, each exactly one hour including Q&A
- Each talk has:
  - A single speaker (some speakers give 2 talks)
  - A track: backend, frontend, data, DevOps, security, or career
  - An expected audience size from pre-registration (ranging from 20 to 280)
  - A difficulty level: introductory, intermediate, or advanced
- 3 talks are designated as keynotes — one per day
- 5 talks are designated as workshops — they must be in small rooms (Gamma/Delta/Epsilon) because they're interactive and need whiteboard access
- 2 talks require live-streaming and must be in the Main Hall (which has the streaming equipment)
- 8 speakers have availability restrictions:
  - 3 speakers are only available on Day 1
  - 2 speakers can only present in the afternoon (they arrive after lunch)
  - 3 speakers are only available on Day 2 or Day 3
- 12 speakers are giving 2 talks each — the rest give 1
- A few talks have content dependencies: talk B is a follow-up to talk A and should be scheduled after A (same day or later day). There are 6 such dependency pairs

Sponsor requirements:
- 4 talks are sponsor-funded and must be in a room with at least 150 seats (Alpha, Beta, or Main Hall)
- Sponsor talks from competing companies (there are 2 competing pairs) must not be in the same time
- The Day 2 lunch session in Main Hall is a sponsor event, so Main Hall is unavailable during the 12:00-13:00 slot on Day 2

Scheduling rules:
- No speaker can give two talks in the same time
- No room can have two talks in the same time
- Keynotes must be in the Main Hall and in the first slot (9:00) of their respective day
- A talk's expected audience must not exceed the room's capacity. Ideally, the room should not be more than 2x the expected audience either (avoid 30 people in a 300-seat hall)
- Two talks from the same track should not be in the same time, so attendees interested in a track can attend all of them
- Speakers giving 2 talks must have at least one free slot between them for rest and preparation
- Dependency ordering must be respected: if B depends on A, then A's slot must be strictly earlier
- Workshops cannot be in the last slot of the day (16:00) because they tend to run over and the venue closes at 17:30
- No track should have more than 2 talks in a row without a break (to avoid track fatigue)

Goals (in priority order):
1. All 80 talks scheduled, no room or speaker conflicts (mandatory)
2. Keynotes in Main Hall at 9:00 (mandatory)
3. Room capacity not exceeded (mandatory)
4. Speaker availability respected (mandatory)
5. Dependency ordering respected (mandatory)
6. No two same-track talks in the same slot (mandatory)
7. Place popular talks in adequately sized rooms (desirable)
8. Minimize room size waste (don't put 30 people in a 300-seat room) (desirable)
9. Spread each track's talks across different days, not just different slots (desirable)
10. When consecutive talks in the same track exist, prefer them in the same room or same floor to reduce attendee walking (desirable)
11. Honor speaker time-of-day preferences when possible (desirable)
12. Place introductory talks earlier in the day and advanced talks later, when possible (desirable)
