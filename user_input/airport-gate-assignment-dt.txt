I work in operations at a mid-sized international airport and need to assign arriving and departing flights to gates throughout the day.

Airport layout:
- 32 gates total, split across 3 terminals:
  - Terminal A (gates A1-A12): domestic flights only, all gates have jet bridges
  - Terminal B (gates B1-B12): domestic and international, all gates have jet bridges, gates B9-B12 have customs/immigration access for international arrivals
  - Terminal C (gates C1-C8): regional/commuter flights, mix of jet bridge gates (C1-C4) and remote stand gates (C5-C8) requiring bus transfer
- Gates have size categories based on the largest aircraft they can accommodate:
  - Small: regional jets and turboprops (e.g. CRJ-200, ATR-72) — gates C1-C8
  - Medium: narrow-body jets (e.g. A320, B737) — gates A1-A12, B1-B8
  - Large: wide-body jets (e.g. A330, B777, B787) — gates B9-B12 only
- Adjacent gates share ramp space. When a large aircraft is parked at certain gates, the neighboring gate cannot be used simultaneously. The specific conflict pairs are: (B9,B10), (B11,B12), (A1,A2), (A11,A12)

Flights:
- Approximately 180 flights per day (90 arrivals, 90 departures), but arrivals and departures are paired — most aircraft arrive and then depart as a different flight number
- Each flight has: airline, flight number, aircraft type, scheduled arrival time, scheduled departure time, origin/destination, domestic or international flag, estimated passenger count
- Turnaround time between arrival and departure varies by aircraft type:
  - Regional: minimum 25 minutes
  - Narrow-body: minimum 35 minutes
  - Wide-body: minimum 60 minutes
- During turnaround, the gate is occupied (cleaning, refueling, catering, boarding)
- Some flights are overnight stays — aircraft arrives in the evening and departs next morning. These should be assigned remote stands (C5-C8) when possible to free up jet bridge gates

Airlines and preferences:
- 6 airlines operate at this airport
- Each airline has a preferred terminal and set of gates where their ground crew and equipment are stationed
- Airline A is the hub carrier and operates 60% of flights — they strongly prefer Terminal A and gates B1-B8
- Low-cost carriers prefer remote stands (cheaper fees) when their aircraft type allows it
- Codeshare flights (operated by one airline, sold by another) should be at a gate that has signage for the operating airline

Passenger connections:
- About 20% of passengers are connecting between flights
- When two flights have significant connecting passenger flow (provided as a connection list with passenger counts), the gates should be close together
- "Close" means same terminal or adjacent gates — transferring between Terminal A and Terminal C is the worst case (15-minute walk)
- International-to-domestic connections must go through Terminal B gates B9-B12 for arrival (customs) and then transfer to Terminal A for the domestic departure
- Minimum connection time: 30 minutes for domestic-to-domestic, 60 minutes for international-to-domestic. Gate assignment cannot make a legal connection impossible by placing gates too far apart given the walk time

Gate occupancy:
- A gate can only hold one aircraft at a time
- Buffer time between consecutive aircraft at the same gate: at least 10 minutes after one aircraft pushes back before the next can begin docking
- No aircraft may be assigned to a gate where its wingspan exceeds the gate's size category
- If an aircraft arrives early or late (delay), the gate must still be available — schedule should have enough buffer to handle this

Special rules:
- VIP flights (government, charter) must be assigned to specific gates (A1 or A2) regardless of airline preference
- Flights with passengers requiring special assistance (wheelchair, medical) should be at jet bridge gates, not remote stands
- Cargo-heavy flights (belly cargo) should be at gates with direct ramp access to the cargo facility — gates A10-A12 and B9-B12
- Red-eye arrivals before 6:00 AM should use gates farthest from residential areas (Terminal C) for noise reasons
- During peak hours (7:00-9:00 AM and 5:00-8:00 PM), no gate should sit empty for more than 45 minutes — maximize throughput

Goals (in priority order):
1. Every flight must be assigned exactly one gate that fits its aircraft type (mandatory)
2. No two aircraft at the same gate at the same time, respecting buffer times (mandatory)
3. International flights must use gates with customs access (mandatory)
4. Adjacent-gate conflicts for large aircraft must be respected (mandatory)
5. Minimum turnaround times must be met (mandatory)
6. Minimize total passenger walking distance for connections (desirable)
7. Assign airlines to their preferred gates and terminals (desirable)
8. Keep overnight aircraft on remote stands (desirable)
9. Maximize gate utilization during peak hours (desirable)
10. Minimize the number of bus transfers (remote stand assignments) for passenger flights (desirable)
