I run the fulfillment floor at an e-commerce warehouse and need to plan daily pick routes for a team of warehouse pickers.

Warehouse layout:
- Three temperature zones:
  - Ambient: 20 aisles, standard storage for most products
  - Chilled (2-8°C): 6 aisles, dairy, fresh food, pharmaceuticals
  - Bulk: 8 aisles, oversized items stored on pallets rather than shelves
- Each aisle has shelving on both sides, with bin locations identified by zone-aisle-shelf-position codes (e.g. AMB-04-C-12)
- Aisle width varies: ambient aisles are one-way (pickers must travel the full length), bulk aisles are two-way
- There is a single packing station at the front of the warehouse where all routes start and end
- Travel time between bins depends on zone distance and aisle layout — the chilled zone is at the far end, roughly 3 minutes walk from packing
- There is a doorway between ambient and chilled that requires badge scan and takes about 20 seconds to pass through
- Bulk zone requires a different cart type (flatbed), so a picker doing bulk items makes a separate trip from ambient/chilled items

Orders and items:
- Approximately 200 customer orders per day, each containing 1 to 15 items
- Each item is mapped to a specific bin location in a specific zone
- Items have a weight (ranging from 50g to 12kg) and dimensions
- Some items are flagged as fragile (glassware, electronics)
- Some items are flagged as hazardous (cleaning chemicals, batteries) and cannot be on the same cart as food items
- Each order has a priority tier:
  - Express: must be picked by 10:00 AM
  - Standard: must be picked by 14:00
  - Economy: must be picked by 18:00
- Each order has a packing type: standard box, insulated box (for chilled items), or oversized (for bulk)

Pickers:
- Team of 12 pickers working a single 8-hour shift (8:00-16:00)
- Each picker has a standard cart with a weight capacity of 25 kg and space for at most 30 items per trip
- Flatbed carts for bulk zone have a weight capacity of 80 kg but only hold 8 oversized items
- Pickers have different walking speeds based on experience level:
  - New hires (< 3 months): ~30% slower
  - Experienced: baseline speed
  - Veterans (> 2 years): ~10% faster due to route familiarity
- A picker makes multiple trips per shift, returning to the packing station after each trip
- Each picker is trained for all zones, but only 4 pickers are certified to operate the powered pallet jack needed for heavy bulk items (> 30 kg)

Rules:
- Orders should not be split across multiple pickers when they fit on a single cart
- Large orders that exceed one cart load must be split, but all splits should go to the same picker across consecutive trips
- Chilled zone picks must be completed within 15 minutes of entering the zone — if a route has chilled items, those are picked last right before returning to packing, so they spend minimum time at ambient temperature
- Fragile items must be picked last in the route so they sit on top of the cart and aren't crushed
- Hazardous items and food items cannot be on the same cart in the same trip
- A picker cannot carry items from more than 2 zones in a single trip (no ambient+chilled+bulk)
- Bulk items must be picked on a flatbed cart in a separate trip from ambient/chilled items
- Heavy bulk items (> 30 kg) can only be picked by pallet-jack-certified pickers
- Each picker gets a mandatory 15-minute break after every 90 minutes of continuous picking, and a 30-minute lunch break between 11:30 and 13:00
- Express orders take priority — they must be assigned to pickers starting their first or second trip of the day

Goals (in priority order):
1. All orders picked before their priority tier cutoff time (mandatory)
2. All cart capacity and weight limits respected (mandatory)
3. Cold chain integrity maintained — chilled items within 15-minute ambient exposure (mandatory)
4. Hazardous/food separation respected (mandatory)
5. Minimize total walking distance across all pickers (desirable)
6. Balance workload (total items and total trips) evenly across the team (desirable)
7. Minimize the total number of trips needed (desirable)
8. Keep full orders together on one cart when possible (desirable)
9. Assign bulk-heavy waves to certified pickers early in the shift when they're fresh (desirable)
