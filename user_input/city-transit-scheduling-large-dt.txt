I work for a mid-sized city transit authority and need to build daily driver and bus schedules for a network of 18 bus routes.

Network topology:
- 18 routes serving roughly 400 stops across the city
- Each route has a defined stop sequence with known travel times between consecutive stops
- 5 routes are classified as "major" (high ridership corridors), 8 as "standard", and 5 as "minor" (suburban feeders)
- 3 of the minor routes are designated school routes with extra service on school days
- Routes operate from approximately 5:00 AM to midnight, but minor routes end service at 10:00 PM
- Some routes share segments — for example route 4 and route 7 overlap for 6 stops downtown. Overlapping segments should not have two buses from different routes arriving at the same stop within 2 minutes of each other (passenger confusion)
- There are 12 designated transfer hubs where passengers commonly switch between routes. Arrival times at hubs should be staggered to allow 3-5 minute transfer windows between connecting routes

Frequency by time of day:
- Peak hours (7:00-9:00 AM and 5:00-7:00 PM):
  - Major routes: a bus every 8 minutes
  - Standard routes: every 12 minutes
  - Minor routes: every 20 minutes
- Midday (9:00 AM - 5:00 PM, excluding peak):
  - Major: every 12 minutes
  - Standard: every 20 minutes
  - Minor: every 30 minutes
- Evening (7:00 PM - 10:00 PM):
  - Major: every 15 minutes
  - Standard: every 25 minutes
  - Minor: every 30 minutes (then service ends)
- Late night (10:00 PM - midnight):
  - Major: every 30 minutes
  - Standard: every 45 minutes
- The first and last trip of each route have fixed departure times published in the timetable — these cannot change
- School routes get 2 additional trips: one at 7:30 AM and one at 3:30 PM on weekdays during the school year. These are on top of the normal frequency

Fleet:
- 85 buses across 3 depots:
  - Depot A (north): capacity 40 buses, located near the start of routes 1-6
  - Depot B (central): capacity 25 buses, located downtown near routes 7-12
  - Depot C (south): capacity 20 buses, located near routes 13-18
- Three bus types:
  - Standard (50 buses): capacity 45 passengers, diesel, all depots have them
  - Articulated (15 buses): capacity 80 passengers, diesel, only at depots A and B, requires special driver certification
  - Electric (20 buses): capacity 40 passengers, battery range 180 km, located at all depots
- Each bus is assigned to a home depot and must start and end its service day there
- Buses have an odometer and scheduled maintenance: some buses have pre-scheduled maintenance on specific days and are completely unavailable. On average, 5-8 buses are out for maintenance on any given day
- Electric buses must not be dispatched if their range would be insufficient to complete the assigned block of trips and return to depot

Electric bus specifics:
- Full charge takes 45 minutes and gives 180 km range
- Partial charge is allowed: 20 minutes gives roughly 80 km
- Charger availability: depot A has 4 chargers, depot B has 3, depot C has 2
- A charger can only serve one bus at a time
- If multiple electric buses need charging at the same time, they must queue — the schedule should avoid overloading charger capacity
- At least 60% of trips on route segments passing through the city center low-emission zone must be served by electric buses (regulatory requirement)
- Electric buses should be prioritized for routes that loop back near their depot, so mid-day charging is feasible
- In winter months, electric bus range drops to roughly 140 km — the schedule should account for seasonal range reduction

Drivers:
- About 110 drivers total
- Each driver has:
  - A home depot preference (where they prefer to start/end their shift)
  - License qualifications: all can drive standard, ~40 have articulated certification, ~30 have EV training
  - A seniority level: trainee (first 4 weeks), junior (< 2 years), senior (2+ years)
- Trainee drivers must be paired with a mentor driver — they cannot operate a bus alone. The mentor must be a senior driver on the same route, driving the bus immediately ahead or behind in the schedule so they can radio for help
- Some drivers have medical restrictions: 6 drivers cannot work shifts starting before 7:00 AM (sleep disorder accommodation), 3 drivers are limited to 7-hour shifts

Driver labor rules:
- Maximum shift length: 9 hours including all breaks and deadheading
- After 4.5 hours of continuous driving, a mandatory 30-minute break is required
  - Breaks can only happen at designated relief points: the 12 transfer hubs, any route terminus, or at depot
  - If no relief point falls naturally at the 4.5-hour mark, the schedule must route the driver to one
- Meal break: 45 minutes unpaid, must occur between the 4th and 6th hour of the shift
  - Meal breaks must happen at a location with facilities (depot or one of 5 designated hubs with a break room)
- Split shifts are allowed: a morning block (e.g. 5:30-9:30) and an evening block (e.g. 16:00-20:00) with a 3+ hour unpaid gap
  - No driver may have more than 2 split shifts per week
  - During the gap, the driver is not on the clock and the bus is available for another driver
- No more than 5 consecutive working days, then at least 1 day off
- At least 11 hours between the end of one shift and the start of the next
- Weekly driving hours (Mon-Sun) must not exceed 45 hours
- A driver must stay with the same bus for their entire continuous driving block (no swapping buses mid-block, though they can swap at the start of a new block after a meal break)

Trip and route rules:
- Every scheduled trip on every route must be covered by exactly one bus and one driver
- A bus can serve trips on different routes throughout the day, but when transitioning between routes:
  - Minimum 5-minute layover at the transfer point for schedule recovery
  - The transition must happen at a stop that both routes share, or the bus must deadhead
- On high-frequency routes (headway ≤ 10 min), buses must maintain headway regularity: actual spacing between consecutive buses cannot deviate more than 20% from the target headway. For example, an 8-minute headway route should have buses spaced 6.4-9.6 minutes apart
- Express trips (which skip some stops) exist on 3 major routes during peak hours. Two express trips on the same route cannot be scheduled back-to-back — there must be a local trip between them to ensure all stops are served

Accessibility and service quality:
- Wheelchair-accessible buses (all articulated and 80% of standard fleet are accessible, all electric are accessible) must serve at least 80% of trips on routes passing hospitals, senior centers, and government buildings — there are 7 such routes
- On school routes, articulated buses are preferred for the 7:30 AM and 3:30 PM extra trips due to high student ridership
- If a route has a scheduled frequency of every 12 minutes or less, the system should detect and avoid "bus bunching" (two buses arriving at the same stop within 2 minutes)

Deadheading:
- When a bus needs to travel empty between routes or to/from depot, this is deadheading
- Deadheading uses fuel/energy, contributes to driver hours, and produces no revenue
- The schedule should minimize total deadheading distance across the fleet
- Deadheading during peak hours should be especially minimized as it wastes a bus that could be in service

Depot operations:
- Each depot has limited parking spaces (see capacity above)
- At any point during the day, the number of buses physically at a depot (parked, charging, or in maintenance) cannot exceed its capacity
- Depot A has a wash bay that can process 1 bus at a time, taking 15 minutes — buses returning after a muddy/rainy day should be scheduled for a wash, but this is low priority
- Shift handoffs happen at depots: when one driver's shift ends and another's begins on the same bus, the handoff takes 5 minutes at the depot

Goals (in priority order):
1. Every scheduled trip on every route is covered by a qualified driver and an appropriate bus (mandatory)
2. All driver labor regulations respected: shift length, mandatory breaks, rest between shifts, weekly hours (mandatory)
3. Bus range and depot assignment rules met — electric buses don't run out of charge, buses return to home depot (mandatory)
4. No charger overload — number of buses charging simultaneously at a depot doesn't exceed charger count (mandatory)
5. At least 60% electric coverage in the low-emission zone (mandatory)
6. Minimum turnaround/layover times between route transitions (mandatory)
7. School route extra trips covered during school days (mandatory)
8. Minimize total number of buses needed in service each day (soft — fleet efficiency)
9. Minimize total deadheading distance (desirable)
10. Maintain headway regularity on high-frequency routes within 20% tolerance (desirable)
11. Keep driver shifts close to 8 hours to reduce overtime (desirable)
12. Fairly rotate split shifts and early-morning starts across drivers using a rolling fairness tracker (desirable)
13. Assign drivers to their preferred home depot (desirable)
14. Prefer electric buses in the low-emission zone beyond the 60% minimum (desirable)
15. Stagger arrivals at transfer hubs for good passenger connections (desirable)
16. Avoid bus bunching on overlapping route segments (desirable)
17. Prefer accessible buses on routes serving hospitals and senior centers (desirable)
