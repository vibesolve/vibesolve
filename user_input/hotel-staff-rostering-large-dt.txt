I manage staffing for a large hotel with around 120 employees across 6 departments. I need to build a 4-week rolling schedule that assigns each employee to shifts.

Departments and shift patterns:

Front desk:
- Three 8-hour shifts: morning (6:00-14:00), afternoon (14:00-22:00), night (22:00-6:00)
- Minimum staffing: 3 morning, 3 afternoon, 2 night on weekdays; 4 morning, 4 afternoon, 2 night on weekends
- Preferred staffing: +1 on each shift above minimums
- At least one staff member on each shift must speak a second language (we serve international guests)

Housekeeping:
- Two shifts: early (7:00-15:00), late (11:00-19:00)
- Minimum staffing: 8 early + 4 late on weekdays, 10 early + 6 late on weekends and holidays
- Preferred: +2 early on checkout-heavy days (Sundays, Mondays)
- Housekeepers are organized into floor teams of 2-3 — team members should be on the same shift when possible
- VIP floor assignments require at least one employee with VIP training certification per team

Food & beverage (F&B):
- Four shifts: breakfast (5:00-13:00), lunch (10:00-18:00), dinner (16:00-24:00), split shift (10:00-14:00 then 18:00-22:00)
- Minimum staffing: breakfast 4, lunch 5, dinner 6 on weekdays; all +2 on weekends; all +3 on holidays
- Split shift is used to cover both lunch and dinner rush — counts as a single shift but the gap (14:00-18:00) is unpaid
- At least one employee on breakfast shift must have food safety certification
- At least one employee on dinner shift must be bartender-certified
- F&B employees should not work breakfast shift the day after a dinner shift (only 5 hours between shifts)

Maintenance:
- Day shift (8:00-16:00) and on-call night shift (16:00-8:00, 16 hours with sleep allowed)
- Minimum: 3 day, 1 on-call on weekdays; 2 day, 1 on-call on weekends
- On-call must include at least one electrician and one general technician
- On-call shifts count as 8 working hours (not 16) for weekly hour calculations
- Maintenance staff cannot do on-call more than 2 times per week

Spa/wellness:
- Single shift (9:00-21:00) with a mandatory 1-hour break between 13:00 and 15:00
- Minimum: 3 staff (at least 1 licensed massage therapist, 1 aesthetician, 1 receptionist)
- Weekend minimum: 4 staff (at least 2 therapists)
- Spa staff work a max of 4 days per week due to physical demands

Events/banquets:
- Variable shifts depending on the bookings calendar, typically 6-10 hours
- Events are known 1-2 weeks in advance with specific staffing needs (e.g. "Wedding reception Saturday 16:00-24:00, need 12 staff including 1 supervisor")
- If an event needs more than 8 staff, a supervisor must be assigned
- Events shifts must be staffed at least 48 hours before the event starts
- Events staff frequently come from F&B cross-training pool

Employees:
- Each employee has a primary department and role/skill set
- About 30 employees are cross-trained and can fill shifts in one secondary department:
  - F&B ↔ Events is the most common cross-training (18 employees)
  - Front desk ↔ Spa receptionist (4 employees)
  - Housekeeping ↔ Maintenance general (8 employees)
- Each employee has contracted weekly hours: 20h (part-time), 30h (three-quarter), or 40h (full-time)
- Part-time employees have specific available days (e.g. "Mon, Wed, Fri only")
- Seniority levels: junior (< 1 year), mid (1-5 years), senior (> 5 years)
- Each employee can submit:
  - Preferred days off (soft, honored when possible)
  - Mandatory days off for medical or personal reasons (hard, must be honored)
  - Shift preferences: a ranked list of which shifts they prefer (desirable)
- Some employees have been flagged:
  - "Nearing retirement" — should not be assigned night shifts or physically demanding shifts
  - "Light duty" (injury recovery) — limited to front desk or spa receptionist, max 6 hours/day
  - "New hire" (first month) — must be paired with a senior employee on at least half their shifts

Shift assignment rules:
- An employee cannot work two shifts in the same day
- After a night shift (ending at 6:00 or later), at least 12 hours off before the next shift
- After any shift ending at 22:00 or later, the next shift cannot start before 8:00
- After a dinner shift ending at 24:00, the next shift cannot start before 10:00
- No more than 6 consecutive working days without at least 1 rest day
- No more than 3 consecutive night shifts
- Every employee must have at least 2 full weekends off per 4-week period (full weekend = both Saturday and Sunday off)
- Contracted hours must be met within a tolerance of +/- 4 hours over each 4-week cycle
- Overtime beyond contracted hours is allowed but capped at 10 hours per employee per month
- Cross-department assignments cannot exceed 3 per employee per month
- An employee cannot be cross-assigned to a department they haven't been trained for
- Employees on light duty cannot exceed 30 hours per week regardless of contract

Fairness rules:
- Unpopular shifts (nights, holidays, F&B breakfast, weekend housekeeping early) are tracked with a fairness score
- The fairness score carries over month to month — employees who did more unpopular shifts last month should do fewer this month
- Night shifts specifically should be distributed so no employee does more than 2 more night shifts than any other eligible employee in the same department over a 4-week period
- Overtime hours should be distributed evenly across eligible employees, not concentrated
- Holiday shifts earn 1.5x fairness credit (so an employee doing a holiday night shift gets more fairness relief next month)
- Weekend shifts should be distributed so every employee gets roughly the same number of weekend days worked

Goals (in priority order):
1. Cover all minimum staffing requirements in every department and shift (mandatory)
2. Respect all mandatory days off, medical restrictions, and light-duty limitations (mandatory)
3. Respect all legal rest requirements: 12h after night, no 6+ consecutive days, etc. (mandatory)
4. Meet contracted hours within tolerance for every employee (mandatory)
5. Respect overtime cap of 10h/month (mandatory)
6. Staff events at least 48 hours in advance with required roles (mandatory)
7. Maximize preferred staffing levels across all departments (desirable)
8. Distribute unpopular shifts fairly using the rolling fairness score (desirable)
9. Distribute overtime hours evenly (desirable)
10. Honor soft day-off preferences (desirable)
11. Honor shift preferences (desirable)
12. Minimize cross-department assignments (desirable)
13. Keep housekeeping floor teams together on the same shift (desirable)
14. Pair new hires with senior employees (desirable)
15. Avoid assigning nearing-retirement employees to night or physical shifts (desirable)
